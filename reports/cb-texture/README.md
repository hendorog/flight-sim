# Cumulonimbus texture: owner screenshots (2026-10-20, main at 05c2b10)

## cb-from-outside.png
C172 chase view, cell about 5-10 km ahead, midday, heading 256.

- Fine, regular diagonal hatching over the whole cell body (about 60 deg, up-left
  to down-right, a few px apart), on both the lit and the shaded faces. It reads as
  a fixed screen- or texture-space pattern, not volumetric detail.
- Flat, blotchy dark mottling across the body, including the lit face; it looks
  like painted 2D noise, with no lit/shaded turret structure.
- Frayed, hair-like fringe on the edges (mostly the lit left edge); nearby cumulus
  has crisp edges.
- A hard, dead-straight dark line along the base; the rain below it is a uniform
  flat grey slab with a straight bottom edge.

## cb-under-base.png
Low-wing T-tail at 950 ft (595 ft AGL), heading 252, just under and inside
the base.

- The same hatching, here like a canvas or woven weave, across the whole base.
- Blue sky shows through holes in the base with sharp, blotchy edges (upper left).
- Mottled light and dark patches, no depth cues.
- Below the base, uniform grey murk with no rain streaks; a hard horizontal edge
  where the base meets the murk.

## cb-exit.png
Cockpit view, flying out from under the cell. The owner says the transition was
clearly visible and was much worse in the few seconds before this shot.

- The cell's murk ends in a hard edge, not a gradual thinning. In the middle of
  the frame a dead-straight vertical boundary runs from the cumulus base up to
  about mid-height. Normal cumulus is on the left of it, and on the right there is
  flat grey-blue haze with no clouds at all.
- Above that, the boundary goes on as a large faint arc across the sky. A second,
  concentric arc sweeps from the upper left down to the lower right of the frame.
  It looks like the camera is looking out through the wall of a cylinder (the
  cell's murk or clearing volume) whose edge has no falloff.
- The cumulus next to the boundary is cut off sharply, with no thinning towards
  the cell.
- Inside the cylinder the ground and sky are washed out evenly. Outside it,
  contrast comes back suddenly.
